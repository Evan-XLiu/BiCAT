Abstract

The action tokenizer is a natural place to model how two arms coordinate.

Autoregressive vision-language-action (VLA) models predict discrete action tokens with the next-token objective of their vision-language backbone, so the action tokenizer defines the interface between the policy and the robot. In bimanual manipulation, the two arms share a repertoire of movements yet coordinate through complementary roles, and an action representation should capture both. However, most existing tokenizers encode both arms as a single concatenated action vector, in which motion patterns common to the two arms are not shared and cross-arm dependencies remain entangled within each token. The policy must therefore learn both aspects implicitly. We propose Bimanual Coordination Action Tokenization (BiCAT), which shares one motion representation across arms while conditioning each arm’s tokens on its partner through symmetric cross-arm attention and a joint decoder. The policy then predicts all tokens of each arm in parallel, generating the left arm first and the right arm conditioned on it. Across ten RoboTwin 2.0 tasks and six real-world tasks on three embodiments, BiCAT achieves the highest success among the compared tokenizers. In simulation, it improves mean success by 17.6 percentage points over the strongest of eight baselines, with the largest gains on tasks that require close coordination between the arms, while requiring only 40.5 ms per action chunk. These results suggest that the action tokenizer is a natural place to model bimanual coordination.

The idea

Keep the arms apart.
Let them talk.

Joint tokenizers entangle both arms inside every token. Per-arm tokenizers keep identity but lose the partner. BiCAT keeps arm-indexed token streams and computes them from both arms.

Overview of BiCAT. Top: mirror canonicalization flips the signed joints of the right arm and applies shared normalization; the shared encoder and causal cross-attention give each arm its partner's context before FSQ; the joint decoder reconstructs the canonical chunk. Bottom: a VLM predicts yL, then yR; the frozen decoder returns actions. Teal: left arm; orange: right arm. PDF ↗

1

Mirror canonicalization

The right arm is flipped by an embodiment-specific sign vector s, and both arms share channel-wise normalization bounds. Mirrored motions, such as reaching inward, map to the same features in either arm.

2

Partner-aware tokens

A shared causal encoder compresses each arm's 32-step chunk into 8 latents. Symmetric causal cross-attention adds partner context before FSQ (K = 1920), and a joint decoder reconstructs both arms from temporally paired codes.

3

Two-block prediction

The VLA predicts all 8 left-arm tokens in parallel, then all 8 right-arm tokens conditioned on them: two decoding steps and 40.5 ms per action chunk.

Analysis

Reconstruction isn't the goal.
Closed-loop control is.

Reconstruction error does not predict success (ρ = −0.13): Bin and FAST reconstruct best yet perform worst. Success instead tracks reliance on vision (ρ = 0.72), consistency across replanning (ρ = −0.80) and smoothness at chunk boundaries (ρ = −0.78), where BiCAT has the smallest jump of the nine tokenizers.
