\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We introduced \textbf{BiCAT}, a bimanual action tokenizer for autoregressive VLAs. \textbf{BiCAT} gives each arm its own tokens, shares one motion representation across the mirrored arms, and conditions each arm's
discretization on its partner; the policy then predicts the tokens of the
two arms in two successive blocks. Experiments in simulation and on three
real bimanual embodiments demonstrate that \textbf{BiCAT} achieves the
highest success among the compared tokenizers, with gains that grow as the
arms become more tightly coupled, while requiring only two decoding steps
per action chunk. Our ablations further show that the interaction between
the arms inside the tokenizer and the block prediction in the policy are
both required for the gain over joint tokenization. These results
highlight the action tokenizer as a natural place to model bimanual
coordination in VLAs.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\textbf{BiCAT} is designed for joint-space actions: its fixed per-joint sign
vector does not directly carry over to end-effector actions, where
mirroring positions and orientations requires a different mapping. Each
tokenizer is also trained for a single embodiment, and each arm receives a
fixed, equal token budget; we leave cross-embodiment and adaptive token
allocation to future work.
